# Changelog

What changed in each release, newest first. The full notes are on the [releases page](https://github.com/LibreGuild/PilotHouse/releases).

Each release also has **Merge notes** for organisations that build their own copy of PilotHouse ([docs/customizing.md](docs/customizing.md)): what to look at when merging it. These cover new `[features]` keys (new features arrive switched on), new or renamed keys in `brand/brand.toml`, changes to `crates/custom` or `Store::apply_track`, and moved files.

## Unreleased

- **The Bridge: every task, by what it needs from you.** The window opens on four columns, *Needs you*, *Working*, *Ready for review* and *Done*, with the chats of every workspace in them (and agent CLIs in terminals, and reviews being readied). A card says which workspace and agent, what the agent is doing, asks or said last; it opens the chat, *Review changes* opens it with what changed beside it, *Done* puts it away. Runs PilotHouse started itself (a schedule's, a test's recording or repair) go to *Done* once read, as they folded into *Runs* before. Chips narrow it to one workspace. After the update your chats of the last week wait in *Ready for review* (older ones are in *Done*); the column's ⋯ marks them all done at once. Before, chats were listed by where they live, under each workspace in a list beside the screen, and the ones that needed something had to be found (the *Agents* pop-up listed them, behind a button). The Bridge takes the place of both. ⌘⇧E and ⌘⇧A show it; the status bar counts *N need you · N working · N to look at* from anywhere.
- **A task is more than its chat.** A card also says the branch its folder is on, what the chat changed (files, lines added and removed), how it checked that (its test commands' last result, screenshots) and, where the repository is on GitHub and an account is connected, the branch's pull request with where its checks stand (it opens in the browser). A chat keeps these numbers with it when a turn ends, so they show for chats that are not loaded too. **A question answered by picking an option is answered on its card** (*Allow once*, *Always*, *Deny*, as the agent gave them), with *Open* for reading the chat first. ***New task* can start one on a branch of its own**: *From <workspace>…* makes the branch and its worktree and opens it, as *New task in a worktree* does from a workspace.
- **One column of icons, with a badge per workspace.** The Bridge, Reviews, then each workspace as its letter on its colour, with a dot for what goes on there and its menu on right-click; **+** and Settings. The list beside it and the column of tool icons on the right are gone, and the room goes to the work. ⌃1–⌃9 (Alt+1–9) still open the first nine workspaces.
- **Chats that need no folder have a fixed place, *Chats*.** At the top of the column of icons, under the Bridge and Reviews, with an icon of its own (⌘⇧D). Underneath it is now a workspace like the others, so its chats get the same screen: tabs, the terminal, the browser, cards on the Bridge. It was a space of a kind of its own with a lesser screen. It is not one of your projects: it is not removed and takes no folder (a folder added from it opens as a workspace of its own), so a quick chat, *New task → Without a folder* and ⌘⇧D always have their place and their chats are always found there. A first start makes it and opens in its chat.
- **A workspace's screen is two panes: the chat and one result pane.** Over the result pane are the names *Git* (what changed, to review and commit), *Preview* (the browser, or a phone), *Terminal* and *Files* (the folder tree with the editor beside it); the board, run configurations, tests, pull requests and the phone's log are under ⋯. The tool in front has the whole pane, so the browser, a phone and the board are no longer squeezed into a column. ⌘B folds the chat to a strip at the edge, ⌥⌘B hides the result pane, ⌘⇧J gives the result pane the window, and ⌘J shows the terminal and goes back. The line between the two panes drags, and stays where you leave it. **This replaces the dock**, where every tool was a tab that could be dragged anywhere: the tool you had in front, whether the pane was open, your chat and your open files come back as they were, but an arrangement of your own (a tool moved beside or under the chat, the bottom panel) does not.
- **Several chats in one workspace are tabs over the chat.** Each chat still open there (it needs you, is being worked on, or waits for a look) has a tab with its state: a click shows it, the tick on a tab marks it done, a right click has its menu. In a narrow panel the tabs are a row of their own, with *Past chats* and *New chat* at its end, over a row for the chat in front (its agent, title, what it has used, and one ⋯ menu for view mode, *Compact chat* and *Continue with*); from 760 px the two are one row. ***Past chats* marks what each chat is doing** (at work, waiting for an answer, stopped, finished and not looked at). The workspace's name and the chat's place no longer stand in the chat's header: the title bar names the workspace.
- **Git: one review of the changes, beside their list.** A row unfolded to its hunks in the list, and a click on it opened the same changes again in the editor. Now the list picks and one diff of every changed file shows beside it (under it in a narrow pane), in one column or side by side. A row starts with a tick for *viewed* where the arrow was: a ticked file folds away in the diff, and a change made after takes the tick off. In the diff: *Undo* and *Keep* on each file, *Keep these lines* / *Discard these lines* under each hunk of a file with several, a note on any line (the **+** beside it; also on a removed line), next and previous change. While an agent writes, the list follows it and the diff says *The files changed since this was drawn* and waits to be asked, so the lines you are reading stay put. Over the changes stands what the chat in front did to check its work: each test command's last result, the commands it ran, the screenshots it took.
- **Reviews is a reviewer's queue** (it was *GitHub*). A list of pull requests is grouped by what an agent has made of each: *Findings to read*, *Agents at work*, *Not started*, *Reviewed, nothing found*. **Tick the ones nobody looked at and start an agent on each at once**; every review runs on PilotHouse's own copy of the repository, only reads, and posts nothing until you do. A row follows its review (*Cloning acme/shop 12s*, then what the agent is doing) and so does a card on the Bridge. *Check the fixes* shows on one the author pushed to since.
- **A pull request's page puts findings first.** Its tabs are *Findings*, *Walkthrough*, *Files*, *Conversation* (it was *Overview*, and came first), *Commits*, *Checks*; with an agent's review at hand it opens on *Findings*.
- **The browser starts when you ask.** Showing *Preview* no longer launches it: the pane says *The browser is not running* with *Start the browser*. It also starts when you enter an address, and when an agent uses it.
- **The window has one background.** *Translucent*, *Mica*, *Gradient* and *Image* (Settings → Appearance → Window, with the panel opacity) are gone: five looks to keep readable in every theme, for a setting few changed. A window that had one is plain now; pictures kept for it are removed from the data folder.
- **The working mark's bars each have a colour**, and with animations switched off in the system (*Animation effects* on Windows, *Reduce motion* on macOS) they light up one after another: the mark stood still there, and read as stuck.
- **Fixed: the window could stall while something was loading.** A loader on screen redraws the views around it on every frame, and several of them looked things up on disk as they drew, which is slow on Windows: whether each agent is installed (a search of `PATH`), an agent's set-up checks, settings. These are remembered for a moment now (*Check again* looks at once). The look every five seconds at stored chats runs off the UI thread, terminals kept from the last run open one at a time, and the form for a new pull request opens before its base branch is found.
- **Fixed: *Restore files* showed at the left edge**, across the chat from the message it belongs to. It is under your message, at the right.
- **Fixed on Windows and Linux: the app's mark in the title bar** stood right of the line the icons under it are on. It is centred over that column.
- **Removing a workspace asks first when it has chats**, and says how many go with it. It went at once before, chats and all.
- **Fixed: a chat opened in PilotHouse from the quick chat showed as a new, empty chat** (or nothing, when its workspace was made by the quick chat): the window reads the workspace's chats, and its workspaces, again for one it does not know. A chat of a workspace shown in another window opens in that window.
- **The branch picker, made over** (the branch at the window's bottom left). One list with each branch once, in place of *Local* and *Remote* tabs that showed every shared branch twice and hid a search's results in the other tab: the local branches, then those on a remote only, folded until wanted. **+** opens a form for a new branch that says what it starts from and what its name will become; **⋯** on a branch gives *New branch from here*, *Rename*, *Merge into* the branch checked out and *Delete*. Deleting a branch with commits of its own says they would be lost and asks, where it failed without a way on. Fetch, pull and push are icons with what they would do in their tooltips and the number of commits beside them, and the branch's standing is under its name.
- **The board is gone.** Each workspace had a *Board* tool: cards in four columns for agents to plan on and work through, with tools for agents (`board_*`), `#12` in a message for a card, and UI tests a card had to pass. The Bridge shows what every chat is doing and what it needs, which is what the board was reached for; a plan is now what an agent writes in its chat. Removed with it: the *Plan … board cards* starters (*Plan a feature* writes a plan), *Make a card* on a failing UI test, and the board's line in the morning-summary schedule. Cards already made stay in the database, unread. ⌘3 is free.
- **A preferred browser for agents** (Settings → Agents → Web pages): *Chrome (PilotHouse Chrome)*, your own Chrome through the extension, or the *Built-in browser*. Chrome is the default. Agents are told which to use as a chat starts, and that Chrome is not connected when it is not, so they start in the built-in browser without failing first; a page asked for in Chrome while it is not connected opens in the built-in browser by itself, and the agent goes on there.
- **The built-in browser opens no DevTools port.** Its menu had *Allow other DevTools clients* and *Copy DevTools address*: an address for tools, which opens to nothing in a browser, for a client agents never needed (they drive that browser through PilotHouse's own tools), behind a port any local process could use. Both are gone; a port left switched on is no longer opened.
- **The Chrome extension is one small popup.** Its toolbar button opened a side panel that held everything the page's right-click menu also had, an options page of switches, and more. Now it opens a popup: *Send to a chat* (the selection, the page, a screenshot, an element, each with its shortcut), *Record* (a bug report, a workflow), *Let agents use this tab*, the chats waiting for you and the agents in the browser when there are any, and where what you send goes. The right-click menu keeps only what a right-click points at. Opening the popup is you pointing at the tab, so the extension no longer asks for sites at all (its optional access to every site is gone from its manifest), and a bug report started from it has its video. It works the same in Chrome, Edge and Brave. **Reload the extension once** at `chrome://extensions` after updating.
- **Removed from the extension:** PilotHouse's buttons on GitHub, Jira and Linear pages; *Send a summary* (it used Chrome's built-in model: what reaches a chat is the page itself); Chrome notifications for chats that finish (PilotHouse posts those); the switches for the toolbar count and the hand-off notification (both are simply on).
- **Names show under the icons at the window's left edge**, for everyone, also after updating: they were off unless switched on. *Settings → Appearance → Names under the icons* switches them off.
- **The accent reaches the buttons and the switches, in every theme.** The main buttons (*Send*, *New task*, *Save*) never took it: they have colors of their own that were not set. Switches, checkboxes and tabs took it only in a theme whose button color is exactly its focus color, so not in One Dark, One Light or Studio Dark. Now the controls that show a choice follow in every theme, and the main buttons wherever the theme draws them in a color (not Modern Light's black). The text cursor follows too.
- **Text on an accent reads.** White was put on the bright green, teal, amber and orange (2.3 to 1 on the green). The text is chosen by how bright the color looks, not by its HSL lightness: ink on those, white on blue, violet, pink and red, whose buttons are a shade darker where white needs it. Every accent's button text now stands 4.5 to 1 or better from its fill. An accent used as text (links, tinted icons) is lightened or darkened until it reads on the theme.
- **An accent no longer recolors states.** It replaced the theme's *info* color, which also says *Working*, a renamed file, a minor finding and an informational diagnostic: a green accent made *Working* look like *Ready for review*, a red one made a note look like an error. States keep the theme's colors; what was tinted only to draw the eye (branch and account icons, chips) follows the accent.
- **Settings → Chrome extension is a guide with drawings.** Each step is a sentence beside a wireframe of where in Chrome it happens (the extensions page with *Developer mode* and *Load unpacked*, the puzzle-piece menu with the pin, the tab group, the right-click menu), with the thing to click ringed in the accent. They are drawn by the app, so they follow the theme and the interface size. A step ticks off when done, and the setup steps fold away once Chrome is connected.
- **Settings, gone through page by page.** Its list fits the window without scrolling (*Appearance* no longer starts unfolded), with what belongs to agents together (*Agents*, *MCP & skills*, *Chrome extension*) and *Templates* named for what it holds, *Review templates*. *Agents* is a line per agent that is ready (it was every check of every agent, several screens of green ticks), the whole card only for one that needs setting up, and has *Check again*. *Features* says what each tool is, and its heading and hint show (they were never drawn). Search finds the pages that are one view (*GitHub*, *MCP & skills*, *Review templates*, *Network drives*): it found none of them. The descriptions are shorter, the quick chat's says where its chats are (*Chats*), and the two update switches are *Check for updates automatically* and *Install updates automatically*.
- **Fixed in Settings: controls that ran off the page.** *Your own theme*'s buttons and, on macOS, the hint of *Open with PilotHouse* are under their text, not beside it. *Network drives* has an icon of its own (it shared *Storage*'s).
- **Fixed: a review template could not be left, and its last lines could not be reached.** A template opened in its row's place among the others, in a box taller than the page, with *Cancel* and *Save* under it where the wheel (which moved the box's own text) never brought them. It now has the page to itself: *← Templates*, *Cancel* and *Save* over its fields, and a box as high as the page has room for. A click on a template's row opens it (the *Edit* button beside it did the same and is gone).
- **The Bridge is a panel on the frame**, as a workspace's screen and Reviews are. It was drawn straight on the frame, its title and first column hard against the column of icons.
- **The command palette is wider** (680 by 460, from 448 by 300) and shows a dozen rows.
- **The status bar** counts the tasks and no longer shows the version (Settings → About has it); the detected stack shows with a workspace in front.
- **The title bar** names what is in front (*Bridge*, *Reviews*, the workspace), offers the next step of the task in front (*Review changes*, or its pull request) and holds the switches of what is shown there; Settings moved to the foot of the column of icons.
- **⌘N on the Bridge** starts a chat in the workspace it is narrowed to, else in the one last in front. A command that acts on a workspace (a new chat, a tool's shortcut) brings that workspace forward first, so nothing happens unseen behind the Bridge or Reviews.
- The command palette's names follow: *Show the Bridge*, *Show Reviews*, *Show Chats*, *Show or hide the chat*, *Show or hide the result pane*, *Let the result pane fill the window* (the old names still find them).
- The website is new: it shows what PilotHouse is, how a task goes and what is in the window, in a few words and with drawings made of HTML in place of screenshots (they went stale with every change of the app's look). The drawings move: a window plays a whole task, the Bridge's cards change columns, and the page takes the accent you pick. The README's screenshots moved from `site/shots/` to `docs/images/shots/`.
- For contributors: `cargo coverage -p <crate>` and `cargo coverage-summary -p <crate>` report line and function coverage of the tests ([docs/testing.md](docs/testing.md)). Code the redesign left unused is removed.

### Merge notes

A theme of yours whose main button is a color (not ink) now takes the accent on that button (`button.primary.background`), also when it is not the color of its focus ring.

## 0.6.4

- **The same look on macOS, Windows and Linux.** PilotHouse now carries its fonts, Inter for the interface and JetBrains Mono (without ligatures) for code, so text has the same shapes and takes the same room on every system; before, each system's own font made the same layout sit differently. *Settings → Appearance → Interface font* still offers the system's font and every installed family.
- **One interface size, 16, on every system** (it was 15 on macOS and Linux, 18 on Windows). At 16 every text size, row and gap is a whole pixel, also on a screen at 150 % or 200 %; the earlier sizes fell between pixels, which a Windows screen at 100 % showed as rows and paddings a pixel off. A size you chose yourself stays; the old defaults move to the new one, once.
- **Interface size scales everything**, as zooming a web page does: icons, agent marks, badges, indents, the file tree's rows, the chat's spacing and reading width, the tool icons' column and dialogs grow and shrink with the text. They had fixed pixel sizes before, so a larger text sat beside small icons in dialogs that were too narrow for it.
- **Workspaces, Chats and GitHub in a column of icons at the left edge**, like the tools' column on the right. Each shows its own list beside the screen in front: the workspaces, the chats outside any workspace (with the pinned ones), or GitHub's inbox and repositories. They shared the sidebar as two stacked panes before, which grew crowded. Pressing the icon in front hides or shows the list. Chats has a shortcut of its own, ⌘⇧D (Ctrl+Shift+D). The title bar names what is in front instead of holding the switch.
- **A frame with islands in it.** The title bar, the two columns of icons and the status bar are now one piece, with no line between them and a little light from above in the theme's own colour. What changes with what you look at, the list and the panes, sits in it as rounded panels with a small gap between them.
- **The frame takes the colour of the workspace in front**: faint, strongest in the title bar and fading down the two edges, with the workspace's dot before its name. A glance at the window's edge says where a new chat would go. Chats and GitHub belong to no workspace and stay plain.
- **New chat and Search chats lead the Chats list only.** Over the workspaces, *New chat* went to whichever workspace was in front without naming it; there a chat starts from the workspace's own row, the chat's header or ⌘N. Search from the Chats list covers every chat of every workspace; beside a workspace it starts with that workspace's chats.
- **Views fit the room they have.** A tool's row of views (Git's Changes, Branch, History and Stashes; a pull request's six tabs) folds the ones not in front to their icons in a narrow pane instead of cutting them off. Rows of named buttons (the board's, the Git list's) fold to icons, the commit row wraps, sentences wrap, and the empty chat's starters stack in one column.
- **Fixed: a narrow chat's starters could not be reached.** Beside a pull request (or at a large interface size) the cards of an empty chat stack in one column; taller than the pane, they were cut off at both ends. The chat's empty states now scroll.
- **Fixed: *Check again* at the bottom of the Pull requests tool** when a folder has no GitHub remote: it sits under the message now.
- **Fixed: the chat came back a sliver after *Reset layout* and a restart.** The saved layout held a guess of the pane sizes, not what was drawn. Layouts saved that way are put right when they are loaded.
- **Diffs are easier to read**, wherever they are listed (the Git tab, commits, stashes, a pull request's files, a chat's edits): both files' line numbers, `+` and `−` in a column of their own, and the words that changed in a replaced line marked more strongly. Long lines scroll sideways instead of being cut, also in a pull request's **Files** tab.
- **A review says at once that it has begun.** *Review with agent* opens the chat with the request and its first step, *Cloning owner/repo…* with the time it has taken, as part of the run; the Agents pop-up lists it as work. Before, a small line over the composer was all that showed, and for a pull request opened from the inbox nothing at all (its chat was not attached).
- **A pull request's page** has one button, *Review with agent*; Watch, Check out, Address comments and Refresh are in its ⋯ menu.
- **The editor's bar** holds the file's path and one ⋯ menu (find, symbols, ask the agent, blame) instead of a row of buttons.
- **Auto save** (Settings → Editor): after a pause in typing, or when you leave the editor. Off by default. It never writes what only you may decide (a file changed or removed on disk, text restored after a crash), and with it on, closing a tab writes the file instead of asking.

## 0.6.3

- **Copy rendered Markdown as rich text.** Text selected in a Markdown document (the formatted editor and the preview) or in a chat reply is copied as plain text and as HTML, so it pastes into Confluence, a mail or a document with its headings, tables, lists and code, as from VS Code's Markdown preview (macOS and Windows).
- **Devices: mirroring no longer slows the phone.** While a phone was mirrored, PilotHouse asked it for its rotation every second (`dumpsys display`), which made the phone itself stutter. A turned screen is now seen in the video, and the phone is asked only then. A still screen also costs the computer less.
- **Files:** a strip under the tree stays free for the folder's menu and for drops, however many files there are; the tree is read again when you come back to the window (for folders whose changes the system does not report, such as network drives) and by a new **Refresh** button; it is drawn again only when a folder's listing changed.
- **A file opened in a window of its own** (from Explorer or Finder) has its **Edit** button for pictures and videos, and now PowerPoint decks: Studio opens without a workspace. The Markdown views and *Format* for JSON are there too.
- **Helper processes have names.** The MCP bridge of each agent session and the Antigravity bridge show as `pilothouse-mcp` and `pilothouse-agy-acp` in Task Manager and Activity Monitor, not as more copies of `pilothouse`.
- **Windows fixes:** *Show in Explorer* works for files and folders below the workspace's top folder (and copied paths use `\` throughout); Studio's title-bar buttons (**Export…**, undo, redo, ⋯) take clicks; a video export no longer ends with "access is denied" (the finished file is closed before it is moved, the move is tried again for a moment, and a file that is open elsewhere is left alone, the result saved beside it).
- **Browser:** a stopped browser says it is stopped, with **Start the browser**, instead of "Starting the browser…" with a spinner that never ended; the menu's Stop also stops a browser that is still starting. Agents are told that the built-in browser is driven with PilotHouse's own browser tools (an agent with another browser or REPL tool of its own tried that one, and failed).
- **Lighter while agents work.** The Git tool read the whole working tree (three `git` runs, one a full `git diff`) on every change to a file, shown or not, for every workspace with its Git tool opened once. It now does so only while it is on screen, at most every five seconds for the count on its icon, and not at all for a workspace that is not in front; reads no longer run back to back while files keep changing, and one that finds nothing new draws nothing. Git and the file in front are also read once when you come back to the window.
- **Lighter in the background.** With PilotHouse behind another program, the status bar's look at ports and CI runs once a minute instead of every ten seconds, and no longer starts `git` each time to see whether the commit changed. On Windows it started `netstat` twice and `tasklist` every ten seconds for ports it could never show there; it no longer does.
- **Fixed: the app's log lost to other runs.** Opening a file with PilotHouse from Explorer or Finder, the quick chat shortcut, `pilothouse --version` and `--help` each started a new log file, moving the running app's log along until what it had written was gone. Only the app itself starts its log now.
- **Fixed:** opening a tool from the icon bar while the Chat tab was in front no longer brings the Editor tab forward; the Agents pop-up scrolls when its list is longer than the window.

## 0.6.1

- **Builds for organisations.** Everything an organisation changes now lives in a few places of its own. [docs/customizing.md](docs/customizing.md) explains each:
  - `brand/` holds the name, ids, icons, features on or off, and update sources, read by the app and by the packaging scripts.
  - `crates/custom` holds the organisation's own views beside Workspaces and GitHub, its commands and its startup code.
  - `CUSTOM.md` holds the organisation's rules for coding agents.
- **Coding agents get context.** [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) (and `CLAUDE.md`) covers how the code is laid out and its rules, and a `merge-upstream` skill takes in a new PilotHouse release.
- An organisation's tables go on a migration track of their own (`Store::apply_track`), so they never take PilotHouse's numbers.
- Appearance preferences that a build does not know (a newer one's, or an organisation's) are kept when it saves.
- `scripts/package/icon.py` now makes every icon from one picture (it used to draw an old version of the icon).
- **A new Walkthrough for pull requests.** For the tech lead or manager who reviews a change: a briefing in plain words, written by an agent that read the change and the code around it. It covers what changes and why, how that part of the system works today, what is different after (with diagrams), how it is done, what it affects, where to look closely, how it is tested and what to ask the author. Its text can be selected and copied, and references to code open in the Files tab or on the web. It replaces the gist, file groups and tour, and the tour strip in the Files tab; a walkthrough made by an earlier version is asked for again.
- **Cline.** Cline's CLI joins Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI and Antigravity, with your own install and sign-in (`cline --acp`): chats with its models and its Plan and Act modes, PilotHouse's MCP tools and yours, one-off jobs like commit messages, its sessions in the usage dashboard, and Cline in a terminal with its past sessions to resume. It runs as in your terminal: on a provider Cline uses there that needs no key (Vertex AI, Ollama), not only on a Cline account. Where Cline's ACP side falls short, PilotHouse works around it: attachments go as text, tool titles that are JSON read as words, a reopened chat is recapped to a new session, it neither updates itself nor leaves a background hub running, and an expired sign-in says so with a way to sign in again.
- **Quick chat.** A small chat window over every app, from anywhere: ⌥Space on macOS, Alt+Shift+Space on Windows and Linux (set it in Settings → Chat), the command palette, or `pilothouse quick-chat` for a shortcut in the system's own settings (the only way on Wayland). It moves, resizes and comes back where it was, with the chat it showed; its chats are under Chats. [docs/features/quick-chat.md](docs/features/quick-chat.md)
- **Agents in a terminal stay in the chat list.** A coding agent's CLI run in a workspace terminal is saved as a chat once it is asked something. With its terminal open, clicking it shows the terminal; once closed, it shows the agent's last answer under its title, its session read only in the chat column, with *Continue in terminal* (the CLI resumes it) and *Continue in a new chat*, and Search chats finds what was said in it. Closing a terminal whose agent is at work or waiting for you asks first. Cline in a terminal is followed too, its transcript readable. Cline's provider can be chosen in the chat (⋯ → Provider): its accounts, and the providers saved with `cline auth` that need no key.
- **Clearer agent errors.** When an agent fails a request with a reason (Cline's "Internal error" carries one), the chat shows the reason. A sign-in that expires mid-chat offers the agent's sign-in, and a chat whose agent could not resume its session (a failed load) now gives the new session the chat's history. A remembered model the agent no longer offers is not asked for again.
- **Diagrams zoom.** Hovering a Mermaid diagram, in a chat, a pull request's walkthrough or a Markdown document, shows buttons to zoom out, zoom in, fit, open it large in a dialog and copy its source; a pinch or ⌘/Ctrl and the wheel zoom too.
- **Lighter while agents work.** An agent working in a terminal no longer redraws every window four times a second, nor writes to the database each second; the sidebar shares its chat lists instead of copying them on each draw, and the Chats list shows its 50 newest (*Show all* for the rest). A closed terminal's session file is not looked for again and again, a quick chat window saves where it is once it stops moving, and Cline's settings are read once per change, without waiting for the login shell.
- **Fixed: a database backup of one kind deleting the other.** Backups made before a migration and those made before an organisation's own migrations are now kept apart, three of each.
- **Fixed: Cline's provider changed in one workspace** now starts Cline again in every workspace, so none keeps running on the old one.
- **Fixed: agent processes left running.** A new chat that was replaced before its first message, or switched to another agent, left its agent session open until PilotHouse quit. The same happened to deleted chats and to chats let go of as idle. For Claude Code that is a process of its own with its MCP servers, hundreds of MB each. Sessions now close with their chat (`session/close`, where the agent offers it), and *New chat* on a still untouched chat keeps it.
